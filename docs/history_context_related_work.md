# Previous answers and renewed visual evidence

Primary-source check: **26 September 2026**. This note positions a proposed history-context diagnostic on the same 100 development reports. It contains no new experiment results and establishes neither novelty nor an internal model mechanism. The two 2026 preprints below use related image-swap settings; they are not independent evidence from our document-reading task.

## Closest verified work

**Are VLMs Seeing or Just Saying? Uncovering the Illusion of Visual Re-examination** (arXiv:2605.15864v1, May 2026). VisualSwap replaces an image after initial reasoning and asks whether the model uses the changed evidence. Its multi-turn user instruction substantially improves task accuracy over continuing a self-reflective assistant prefix in the studied Qwen3-VL configurations. Appendix F specifies full re-prefill, avoiding stale visual-cache reuse; Appendix H finds that structural markers alone do not reproduce the improvement from a meaningful instruction in a new turn. This makes conversational presentation a concrete experimental variable. However, image swapping changes the correct answer and retains prior reasoning; revealing a sharper region of the same report with a short previous answer is a different intervention. The paper's attention observations do not establish the mechanism of any LookAgain result. [Paper, §5.2 and Appendices F/H](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.15864v1).

**Recompute or Reuse? Diagnosing and Mitigating Textual Shortcuts in VLM Self-Reflection** (arXiv:2608.01930v1, August 2026). This is especially close: it separates evidence-bearing prior reasoning, explicit final-answer spans, and length-matched non-evidence removals. Evidence removal changes prior-answer preference more consistently than removing the final answer alone; weaker prior-answer preference need not mean a correct replacement. A neutral-history control preserves extra dialogue structure while excluding concrete stale visual premises, and its continuation results differ from prior-conditioned history. Thus, history-content versus generic-history controls already have precedent. The study primarily uses counterfactual images and a model-dependent cohort requiring direct correctness on both images. Its full reasoning histories and selected cohort limit transfer to naturally mistaken short answers on unchanged documents. [Paper, §3 and Appendices B.3/B.6/D.3](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.01930v1).

**VISCO: Benchmarking Fine-Grained Critique and Correction Towards Self-Improvement in Visual Reasoning** (CVPR 2025; arXiv:2412.02172v2). Model-generated critiques can fail to improve an answer. LookBack converts claims in an initial response into questions, checks them against the image, and combines the answers into a critique; this improves critique and correction in its evaluated setting. It motivates checking prior visual claims rather than assuming a second pass helps. It does not isolate native crop detail or answer-history content, and its stated limitation includes reasoning chains capped at five steps and uncertain generalization to complex multi-turn use. [Paper, §5 and Appendices E/F](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.02172v2).

## Implications for the proposed diagnostic

Prior-answer content and chat packaging are distinct experimental factors. The studies motivate the following controls for the proposed history-context diagnostic:

- Compare the actual previously generated answer with an answer-free history under the same valid turn structure, image sequence, overview repetition, follow-up wording, and generation settings. Retain the actual model answer without consulting the target. Record text-token counts; a neutral placeholder can itself carry meaning and differ in length. A length-matched neutral sensitivity check would address one remaining alternative explanation.
- Inspect the rendered chat template, role delimiters, and visual inputs. An empty assistant message must not silently disappear or merge turns. If it does, that branch changes packaging as well as answer content. Re-prefill consistently or document any cache differences.
- Treat a fresh joint overview-plus-region presentation as a useful combined baseline. Its difference from an actual-answer conversation includes both history and presentation; it cannot alone identify an answer-content effect.
- Cross history conditions with native-detail versus degraded-detail versions of the same region, holding placement and dimensions fixed where feasible. Compare the paired native-minus-degraded benefit between histories. This estimates an interaction for the chosen prompts and images, not proof of an internal anchoring mechanism.
- Report all 100 reused reports as the development cohort. Analyze retained answers separately when the initial answer was correct or incorrect, with explicit denominators: retaining a correct answer is desirable. Any initial-error subgroup is outcome-selected. Keep report-level pairing and do not claim held-out generalization.

The proposed hypothesis is that the content of a previous answer reduces the benefit of newly available visual detail compared with an answer-free history. No effect, an opposite effect, or an effect explained by presentation remain plausible. In particular, the closest prior work cautions that a short final answer may behave differently from an evidence-rich reasoning trace. This is a narrow diagnostic motivated by existing work, not an established mechanism or a demonstrated novel method.
